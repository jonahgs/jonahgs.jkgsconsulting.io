Discuss a practical AI project

A useful first project includes

  • One task and an agreed success measure
  • A review of inputs and data permissions
  • A small working pilot
  • A person responsible for checking the output
  • Guidance for using and correcting it
  • A review of time, quality, and running cost

A practical example

A report draft,
ready for a person to review.

A useful workflow makes the source information and review step visible. This example shows the kind of process we might build.

01 / Gather

Start with the sources.

Bring together the agreed operating figures and notes. Flag information that is missing or out of date.

02 / Prepare

Draft the useful parts.

Prepare a summary, organize exceptions, and link back to the source material for checking.

03 / Review

Let the owner decide.

A person checks the figures, corrects the interpretation, and decides what to share or act on.

Illustrative workflow. The pilot would use agreed data and a defined review process.

Before we automate

Make the useful part clear.

What if the process itself needs fixing?

We start there. An unclear decision, missing information, or poor handoff can make automation add work. The initial review decides whether a process change should come first.

Will AI make decisions on its own?

We agree exactly what the system may do and which outputs require review. The responsible person, correction path, and limits are part of the workflow design.

What happens to business information?

Before choosing a tool, we agree what information it needs, who may access it, and which provider or system would process it. A pilot starts with suitable sample or approved data.
